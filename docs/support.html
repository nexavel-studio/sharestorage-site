Setup

Any Android phone running Android 8.0 (Oreo) or later can be the vault. It doesn't need to be fast — even old budget phones work well. The more internal storage the phone has, the more you can back up. The phone should stay connected to power and WiFi.

Yes — the vault runs a server that client phones connect to when they want to sync. If the vault phone is off or offline, sync attempts from a client will fail. We recommend keeping it plugged in and connected to WiFi at all times. If the vault was offline and comes back up, any family member can simply tap Sync now to resume — nothing is lost between offline periods.

No — each installation runs in one mode: Vault or Client. You need at least two phones: one to be the vault and one (or more) to be clients. Most people use an old phone as the vault and their current phone as the client.

Yes — there are two recovery paths. On the login screen, tap "Forgot password?":

  • —Recovery code: Enter the 24-character code that was shown when you first set up your account. Always available.
  • —Device challenge: If you're on the same device where you first logged in, the app can verify your identity via a stored key — no code needed.

After recovery your password is reset. However, your existing backed-up files won't be accessible until a vault admin re-invites you to each storage — this is a deliberate trade-off to protect your data. If you lose both your password and your recovery code, account recovery is not possible.

Connection

ShareStorage communicates directly between devices over your local network — there is no relay server. Both phones need to be on the same local network so they can discover each other. This is what makes it private: your files travel only over your own WiFi, never over the internet.

Not currently. Backup only happens when both phones are on the same local WiFi network. When you get home and reconnect to your home WiFi, open the gallery and tap Sync now to back up everything taken while you were away.

Check these in order:

  • —Both phones are on the same WiFi network (not one on 2.4GHz and one on 5GHz if they're isolated).
  • —The vault server is running — check for a green "ONLINE" indicator in the vault app's Server Status card. The server runs as a background service and doesn't need the vault app to be in the foreground.
  • —Your router does not have AP isolation enabled (this blocks device-to-device communication).
  • —ShareStorage has local network permission on both phones (Android Settings → Apps → ShareStorage → Permissions).

Storage & Files

As much as the vault phone's free storage space allows. ShareStorage has no artificial limits — if your vault phone has 128 GB free, you can use all of it. You can set voluntary per-storage capacity caps in the vault settings to reserve space for other uses.

Photos and videos from your Android photo library — JPEG, PNG, HEIC, MP4, MOV, and other formats supported by Android's media store. Raw camera files (DNG) are also backed up if your camera app writes them to the photo library.

Sync pauses and you'll see a warning in both the vault and client apps. No files are deleted automatically. To resolve it, free up space on the vault phone (delete other apps, clear cache) or archive older backups. Sync resumes automatically once space is available.

No — ShareStorage never deletes files from your phone. It only copies them to the vault. Your originals stay exactly where they are on your device.

Security

Yes. Files stored on the vault are encrypted using AES-256 before being written to disk — photos and documents use AES-256-GCM, videos and audio use AES-256-CTR. Each storage has its own encryption key. That key is wrapped by a key derived from your account password; the derived key is never persisted anywhere — it only exists in memory during an active session. Without your password, the encrypted files are unreadable even with physical access to the phone's storage.

No. Communication between the client and vault uses TLS (HTTPS) with certificate pinning, which means even someone monitoring your WiFi traffic cannot read the contents of the transfer. The connection is encrypted in transit and the files are encrypted at rest.

ShareStorage accesses your photo library only to perform the backup you set up. It does not send any photos to us or any third party — they go only to the vault device you control. Android's permission system will prompt you before access is granted, and you can revoke access at any time in Android Settings.

Family Sharing

There's no hard limit. In practice, performance depends on the vault phone's hardware. A modern mid-range phone handles a family of 5–6 members comfortably. You can create multiple storages with different member lists — for example a personal storage and a shared family storage.

Yes — each storage has a capacity cap you set when creating it, and you can change it later from the storage manage sheet on vault home. When a person has their own private storage, that cap limits how much they can back up. When a storage's cap is reached, uploads to it are paused until the cap is raised or files are deleted from within that storage.

It depends on the storage type. A Private storage is only visible to the one person it belongs to. A Shared storage is visible to all its members — everyone added to it can browse and upload files there. The vault operator controls who is added to each storage.
